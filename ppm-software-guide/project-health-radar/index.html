How to Interpret a Project Health Radar: The 5-Axis Spider Chart Guide

Traditional Red-Amber-Green (RAG) status hides critical warning signs behind subjective colors. Discover how a multi-variate spider chart exposes deliverable equilibrium, team burnout, and hidden delivery risks.

📅 Updated for 2026
·
⏱️ 9 min read

🎯 Technical PMO & Executive Steering Guide

💡 Key Takeaways for PMOs & Steering Committees

  • Beyond One-Dimensional RAG: A single green or amber badge cannot convey whether project progress is healthy or achieved by burning out engineers and ignoring technical debt.
  • Multi-Variate Equilibrium: The 5-Axis Project Health Radar (formally a Radar Chart, Spider Chart, or Kiviat Diagram) evaluates Schedule, Cost, Team Sentiment, Risk Exposure, and Output % on normalized axes radiating from a center point.
  • Polygon Geometry Reveals Truth: A balanced, symmetrical pentagon represents sustainable delivery. Severe asymmetry highlights structural delivery crises before deadlines are missed.
  • Defeating the Watermelon Syndrome: Projects that appear "Green" on high-level executive decks but are secretly "Red" beneath the surface are immediately exposed when the Team Sentiment and Risk Exposure vertices collapse inward.

🚦 1. The Failure of Traditional RAG: The "Watermelon Project" Problem

For decades, enterprise PMOs have relied on Red-Amber-Green (RAG) indicators to report project status to steering committees. While intuitive, single-variable RAG status introduces two fatal governance flaws:

Failure Mode 1
Subjective "Watermelon" Masking

A project manager under pressure will mark an initiative "Green" simply because the scheduled go-live date is still three months away. Yet behind that green badge, technical shortcuts are piling up, key personnel are quitting, and budget reserves are exhausted. It is green on the outside, but deep red on the inside.

Failure Mode 2
Lack of Actionable Diagnostics

When a sponsor sees an "Amber" badge, they cannot tell what action is required. Is the project struggling with supplier budget overruns? Are external dependency milestones slipping? Or is team morale collapsing under unrealistic expectations? One color cannot tell you where to apply executive leverage.

To solve this, modern Project Portfolio Management (PPM) systems like PPM Compass 360 supplement status registers with a 5-Axis Project Health Radar.

📐 3. Understanding the 5 Health Dimensions

In the PPM Compass 360 governance engine, the five axes are mathematically normalized on a scale from 0 (Critical Failure) to 100 (Optimal Health). Here is how each axis is measured and calibrated:

1 Schedule (Milestone Slip vs. Baseline) Temporal Dimension

Measures milestone completion against the baseline commitment. Rather than simply tracking elapsed calendar time, this axis penalizes cumulative slip across stage-gate milestones (using Milestone Trend Analysis heuristics). If critical path deliverables move past contractual stage dates, this vertex contracts inward.

2 Cost & Budget (Burn Rate vs. Forecast) Financial Dimension

Evaluates financial performance by comparing actual expenditures plus committed orders against the allocated CAPEX/OPEX budget baseline. An inward score indicates accelerated burn rates, unapproved change orders, or forecast variances exceeding organizational thresholds (typically >10% variance).

3 Team Sentiment (Engagement & Burnout Index) Human Capital Dimension

The most commonly ignored metric in traditional project management. Captures qualitative delivery confidence, psychological safety, and workload pressure gathered during bi-weekly check-ins. When key resources report excessive overtime or lack of stakeholder cooperation, this axis collapses—providing an early warning months before deadlines are blown.

4 Risk Exposure (Weighted Severity & Proximity) Uncertainty Dimension

Calculated as the cumulative weighted sum of unmitigated items in the project RAID register (Probability × Impact). A high score (near the outer rim) indicates all identified risks are minor or have executed contingency plans. An inward collapse indicates active high-severity threats lacking mitigation owners.

5 Output / Scope (Verified Deliverable Progress %) Value Delivery Dimension

Unlike superficial "task count" percentages from operational task trackers, this axis measures verified deliverable completion against agreed business requirements. It answers the fundamental question: "Has the promised business value actually been built, verified, and accepted?"

🔍 4. Polygon Diagnostics: 4 Common Failure Patterns

Experienced PMO leaders and steering committee chairs do not read the radar axis-by-axis; they interpret the overall geometric silhouette. Here are the four standard diagnostic archetypes:

🛡️ Pattern 1: The Balanced Shield (Equilateral)

Visual: Symmetric pentagon reaching near the outer boundary across all five spokes.

Diagnosis: Ideal health. Delivery velocity matches budget forecasts and team capacity. Steering committees should empower the team with full autonomy and avoid unnecessary interference.

⚠️ Pattern 2: The Hero's Collapse (Asymmetric Skew)

Visual: Output and Schedule are high (80-90%), but Team Sentiment and Risk Exposure are severely contracted (<35%).

Diagnosis: A project being driven across the finish line purely by unsustainable individual heroism. A single resignation will cause total project collapse. Sponsor must inject immediate capacity or descope deliverables.

💸 Pattern 3: The Golden Quagmire (Cost Hemorrhage)

Visual: Schedule, Team, and Output are acceptable, but Cost is severely pinched toward the center hub.

Diagnosis: The project is buying its way out of trouble using expensive contractor overtime, premium expediting fees, or runaway cloud compute infrastructure. Requires immediate financial audit and renegotiation.

🐌 Pattern 4: The Analysis Paralysis (Stagnant Core)

Visual: Team sentiment and Cost appear intact, but Output and Schedule are pinned near 20-30%.

Diagnosis: Teams are comfortable, meetings are happening, and money is being spent, but tangible software or business deliverables are not being completed. Indicates lack of decisiveness or excessive scope churn.

📑 5. How Steering Committees Use the Radar in 1-Page Summaries

In enterprise governance, executive sponsors do not have time to read 20-page PowerPoint status packs. The Project Health Radar solves this by condensing complex multi-variate health into a glanceable asset.

In PPM Compass 360, the 5-Axis Health Radar is automatically embedded into the Executive 1-Page PDF Summary alongside:

  • Calculated Overall RAG Status: Derived algorithmically from the worst-performing dimension to prevent manual whitewashing.
  • Historical Directional Arrows (↗, ↘, →): Showing whether team sentiment or risk posture is improving or degrading over time.
  • Upcoming 30-Day Milestone Horizons: Immediately connecting radar stress to upcoming critical delivery gates.
  • Curated Leadership Commentary: Capturing the PM's narrative context without overwhelming executives with noisy task-level updates.
