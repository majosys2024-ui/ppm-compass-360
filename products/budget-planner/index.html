Good budgeting practice, built in

The right level of detail across Idea, Planning, Execution, and Closing

A budget matures with the project. Budget Planner asks only for what each stage needs, and shows only the screens that matter now.

1 Idea · optional

Rough range

A few lines with accuracy bands and uncertainty cones, plus what planning will cost. Sponsor approves to plan.

2 Planning

Bottom-up budget

Work packages and cost items estimated by their owners, spread over calendar months, with a contingency you can explain.

3 Execution

Track & forecast

Green, amber, or red at a glance. Overruns come with a reason. Forecasts and re-baselines go to the sponsor for approval.

4 Closing

Final account

Final cost reconciled against earlier estimates, contingency used and released, and lessons captured for the next project.

✅ Built for busy people

Everyone always knows what to do next

Most budget tools fail because people stop updating them. Budget Planner keeps the work small and puts it where people look.

  • ✓ My Tasks: One list per person across all projects: estimates to give, monthly updates due, estimates to review, and decisions to take.
  • ✓ Next step indicator: Every project screen states what to do now, with a direct link straight to it.

🛡️ Defensible numbers

A contingency your sponsor won't question

Instead of an arbitrary flat percentage, Budget Planner suggests mathematically sound contingency from the quantified risks you listed and how uncertain each estimate is. You decide the figure and record the written rationale.

  • ✓ Risks with a price: Probability × cost impact (EMV) calculates each risk's expected monetary value.
  • ✓ Estimating uncertainty spread: Individual confidence ranges are aggregated into defensible contingency suggestions.
  • ✓ Clear build-up: Cost baseline + contingency + management reserve = total budget, with a written basis for the sponsor.
  • ✓ Monthly cash flow: Spend distributed over calendar months with cumulative S-curves, fiscal year splits, and Opex/Capex classification.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Budget Planner and the early adopter program.

What does "Free for Early Adopters" mean?

Organizations that join during our early adopter window receive full access to Budget Planner without license fees. In return, we ask for your feedback and real-world use cases as we shape ongoing development. Contact us to receive the package.

Do we have to use formal approvals and a sponsor?

No. With Quick start you go straight to the cost plan and, without a sponsor, approve the budget yourself. Use the full lifecycle across Idea, Planning, Execution, and Closing for larger projects that require formal baseline commitments.

Do owners have to report actual costs every month?

Only if you want them to. With Plan only mode, owners keep their plan current when you ask and give the final cost when an item is finished. Your actuals stay managed in your external finance or ERP system.

How is Budget Planner installed?

It is a standard SharePoint Framework package (.sppkg). Your Microsoft 365 administrator uploads it to the App Catalog and adds it to any target site. A site owner then sets up its lists with one click. It works on any modern SharePoint site.

Does Budget Planner send data outside our tenant?

No. It runs 100% clientside in the browser and stores everything in SharePoint lists on your own site. It calls no external servers, APIs, or analytics tracking services.

Can team members work without delete rights?

Yes. Budget Planner features a strict "no delete" architecture: removing an entry marks it obsolete and preserves its full audit history, so team members can work safely with restricted permissions.

How does it relate to PPM Compass 360?

Budget Planner is a standalone app focused on single-project financial lifecycle management. PPM Compass 360 provides multi-project portfolio health, milestone trend analysis, strategic alignment, and steering reviews. Both run natively in Microsoft 365.
